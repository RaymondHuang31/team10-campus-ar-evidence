AR Campus Navigation & Contextual Guide | Table 26 Team 10 | Week 4 (S1 W4), 7 Oct 2026

STATUS: NOT YET RUN. Sections 1-3 are the ready-to-use pilot script and observation sheet; sections 4-5 must be completed after a real pilot session with one person. Do not report a pilot result that was not actually carried out.

1. What the pilot is for

The Week 4 checkpoint standard requires the instrument to be piloted on at least 1 person, with notes on what must change before Week 5. The pilot tests the instrument - the questions, the wording, the timing and the A/B route - not the participant, and it is never evidence that AR helps users navigate.

2. Pilot run script (follow in order)

0-5 min | Setup. Print the guide, open the note sheet, load both formats on the phone, walk the route once to confirm it is safe and has 2-3 clear decision points.

5-8 min | Consent. Read the learner consent script aloud, confirm the participant agrees (and agrees to notes). Remind them they can stop at any time.

8-23 min | Run the interview (G1-G5) exactly as written. Do not improvise or explain the questions. Circle any question the participant asks you to repeat or misunderstands, and note how long each answer takes.

23-38 min | Run the A/B route task (T1-T6). Use format A (phone map) then format B (mock-up), or swap if this is a second run. Record the three measures for both formats. Note how hard it is to record the measures while walking.

38-40 min | Ratings and debrief. Ask R1-R4, then ask directly: which question was confusing, which sounded like you were being led to an answer, and what would you cut?

After | Complete sections 3 and 4 within 30 minutes while it is fresh; then agree the changes for section 5.

6. Reporting rule

A single pilot is an instrument check only. Pilot findings appear in the log as instrument-quality evidence, and must never be presented as proof that in-view AR cues help users navigate.
