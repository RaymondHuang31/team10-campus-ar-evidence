W4 Instrument v1 - Interview Guides & A/B Route Protocol

AR Campus Navigation & Contextual Guide | Table 26 Team 10 | Week 4 (S1 W4), 7 Oct 2026

Status: DRAFT instrument, not yet piloted. Question order follows the required sequence: warm-up, current behaviour, pain, consequence, existing alternatives, and only last willingness to pay (staff only). Read the consent script (W4_Consent_Data_Plan.docx) before every session.

0. Screening questions (ask before a slot is used)

S1 (students). Have you been studying on this campus for less than one semester?

S2 (visitors / staff). Is this your first visit to this campus, or do you still find it hard to locate buildings you have not visited before?

Record the answer and any refusal, so the screening rate can be reported. Do not interview people who have known the campus for a long time - they are not the target user.

1. Learner / visitor guide (G1-G5, RQ2 / A1, A2) - five of the six steps

Step 1 - Warm-up

G1. Can you tell me what you were doing the last time you had to find a place on this campus that you had not been to before?

Step 2 - Current behaviour

G2. Walk me through what you actually did, step by step - where did you start, what did you look at, and when did you look at it?

Step 3 - Pain

G3. How much longer did it take than you expected, and how many times did you have to stop and check something again?

Step 4 - Consequence

G4. Tell me about a time a campus journey went wrong - a wrong building, a late arrival, a missed meeting. What happened, and what did you do?

Step 5 - Existing alternatives

G5. What do you use today - a map app, campus signs, asking someone? What do you do when you are not sure, and what have you tried that did not work?

Do not ask the learner about price or willingness to pay: learners are not the buyer. Step 6 is asked of staff only (below).

2. Staff guide (G6-G10, RQ3 / A7) - all six steps, price last

Step 1 - Warm-up: G6. What is your role, and what does wayfinding look like from where you sit?

Step 2 - Current behaviour: G7. How is wayfinding supported on campus today - signage, maps, apps, staff at reception - and who is responsible for each?

Step 3 - Pain: G8. What complaints or requests do you receive about finding places, how often, and from whom?

Step 4 - Consequence: G9. What does that cost you - staff time, visitor experience, repeat requests?

Step 5 - Existing alternatives: G10. What have you already tried or considered, and why did it stay as it is?

Step 6 - Willingness to pay (last): G11. If a small pilot were proposed, who would own the decision and the budget, what would a pilot need, what would you compare the cost against, and what would make you say no?

3. A/B route protocol (T1-T6, RQ1 / A3)

T1. Reconfirm consent, explain that the session is about how people follow a route, and confirm the participant can stop at any time.

T2. Use the route chosen by the feasibility audit: one unfamiliar indoor-outdoor route from outside to a specific room, not walked by the participant before. Same route and destination for both formats.

T3. Run it twice: (A) with a normal phone map; (B) with a low-fidelity in-view cue (phone overlay placing the next cue over the camera view, or cues placed at the actual decision points). State before starting that format B is a rough research mock-up, not a product demo.

T4. Counterbalance the order across participants (A first for participant 1, B first for participant 2, and so on) and record the order used.

T5. Record for both runs: total time, number of map checks / look-aways, and wrong turns or hesitations longer than about five seconds.

T6. Ask the rating items below, then close by reconfirming consent and thanking the participant.

Post-task ratings (R1-R4)

R1. How much mental effort did each format take? (1 = very low, 5 = very high)

R2. How confident were you that you were going the right way in each format? (1 = not at all, 5 = completely)

R3. How clear was the guidance at the point where you had to decide which way to go? (1 = very unclear, 5 = very clear)

R4. Which format did you find easier to follow, and what specifically made it easier?

4. Route audit checklist (C1-C8, G12-G13) - supporting feasibility check

C1. Indoor-outdoor transition points on the route.  C2. Signage presence and consistency with the maps.

C3. Outdoor positioning quality and indoor Wi-Fi coverage, including dead zones.

C4. Lighting and glare at the time the route is normally walked.  C5. Stairs, lifts, doors and accessibility.

C6. Crowding at the usual time of day.  C7. Landmark visibility from the decision points.

C8. Any rule or constraint affecting head-worn devices on this route.

G12. How often is this route walked by people who do not know it, and by whom?

G13. What changes to signage or maps are already planned here?

5. Note-taking template (complete immediately after each session)

6. Do-not-ask list (leading wording)

Do not ask: 'Wouldn't AR glasses make this easier?', 'Don't you find it annoying to keep checking your phone?', 'Would you use this if we built it?', or anything that offers the team's product as the answer, and never ask a learner what they would pay. Ask what the person actually did, on which journey, and let the benefit emerge - or fail to emerge.
