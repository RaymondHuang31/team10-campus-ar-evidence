| Assumption ID | Original assumption | Status | Evidence ID(s) | What the evidence says | Decision / revised wording | Risk if wrong | Next test | Owner |
| A1 | New students and first-time visitors can experience difficulty navigating unfamiliar university campuses. | Retained (narrowed) | EV-001; EV-003 | Three problem-focused sources describe indoor-outdoor campus wayfinding difficulty, performance and workload. Supports the problem in general, not its frequency on our campus. | Keep, narrowed to unfamiliar-campus users. | A2 | The problem is mainly a lack of campus information. | Revised | EV-001 | The stronger evidence concerns wayfinding workload rather than missing information. | Revised to: the problem may be translating fragmented maps, signs and directions into real-world movement. | We may design an information app for the wrong problem and the AR justification disappears. | RQ2 - the same interviews test whether the stated cause is missing information or movement. | TBC |
| A3 | AR will automatically be better than a phone map. | Revised - testable hypothesis | EV-002; EV-003 | AR campus systems are plausible and showed results in their own settings, but superiority is unproven for our users, routes and hardware. | Revised to: in-view AR cues may reduce map re-checking, wrong turns and perceived workload for some unfamiliar users on some routes. | Technology-first bias could lead to building AR where a phone map is already sufficient. | RQ1 - counterbalanced within-subject A/B route task (6-8 participants): map re-checks, wrong turns, workload, confidence. | TBC |
| A4 | Users need both indoor and outdoor navigation support. | Retained - design consideration | EV-001; EV-002; EV-003 | All three sources discuss indoor-outdoor or complex-campus navigation and treat transitions as the difficulty. | Keep, but do not build full indoor navigation in the first prototype; treat the transition as the unit of analysis. | Full indoor navigation could expand the scope beyond a single-semester capstone. | Feasibility route audit (supporting activity, not a research question) on 2-3 transitions. | TBC |
| A5 | Rokid is technically suitable for AR navigation / contextual guidance. | Retained - technical feasibility only | EV-004 | Rokid material lists AR navigation as a scenario and describes spatial-computing and developer capabilities; no tracking-performance data. | Keep as feasibility evidence only; confirm hardware and SDK with the mentor and test tracking during the route audit. | Field of view, battery, comfort or indoor tracking may rule out the chosen route. | Mentor confirmation plus device checks in the route audit. | TBC |
| A6 | Users will want historical / tourism information in the first version. | Dropped | No supporting Week 2 problem evidence | Week 2 evidence was stronger for wayfinding than for tourism-content demand. | Removed from the MVP; future extension only if later evidence supports it. | Extra features would distract from the validated problem. | Do not test unless the supervisor asks for the comparison. | TBC |
| A7 | Universities will pay for an AR campus-navigation solution. | Unvalidated | None | No evidence supports willingness to pay.
