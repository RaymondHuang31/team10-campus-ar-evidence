Week 3 USP Hypothesis v2

Rokid AR Campus Navigation & Contextual Guide — corrected 2026-10-07

Status: working market hypothesis. This document replaces the earlier assembly / practical-training USP so that Week 2, Week 3 and Week 4 all use the same campus-navigation scenario.

1. Target user and target customer

Primary user: new university students and first-time campus visitors who are unfamiliar with the campus.

Primary customer / buyer hypothesis: universities or campus-service units seeking to improve wayfinding, onboarding and visitor experience.

Important: the buyer is still unvalidated. Week 4/5 research should distinguish user value from institutional willingness to adopt or pay.

2. Current alternatives

3. USP hypothesis

For new students and first-time campus visitors who currently rely on phone maps, signage or asking for directions, our Rokid-based campus navigation guide is a head-worn AR wayfinding layer that provides campus-specific, in-view directions and contextual information without requiring the user to repeatedly hold and check a smartphone; unlike phone-based tools such as Google Maps Lens or campus map systems, it is designed to keep navigation cues in the user's field of view while moving through the campus.

4. What is actually different?

The proposed differentiator is NOT simply 'AR navigation'. Existing products already provide AR navigation or spatial wayfinding.

The narrower hypothesis is: hands-free, head-worn, campus-specific guidance on Rokid may reduce repeated map checking and uncertainty during unfamiliar-campus navigation.

5. Falsifiable test

The USP should be weakened or rejected if users using the Rokid concept do not show lower map-checking frequency, lower navigation uncertainty, faster task completion or stronger preference than a phone-based baseline.

It should also be weakened if wearing the glasses, hardware availability, indoor positioning limitations or privacy concerns create more friction than the navigation benefit.

6. Evidence required next

• User research: new students / first-time visitors describing recent campus wayfinding behaviour and pain.

• Baseline comparison: how often users check a phone map, ask for directions, miss turns or feel uncertain.

• Concept test: compare a simple Rokid / AR prototype against a phone-map baseline on one or two campus routes.

• Stakeholder research: confirm who inside a university would own the problem and whether the institution would consider adoption or funding.

7. Week 3 decision

Keep campus navigation as the primary use case and retain scenic / tourism guidance only as a later extension. Carry the USP forward as a hypothesis into Week 4 research design rather than presenting it as a validated benefit.
