Week 2 Old-vs-New Problem Comparison

AR Campus / Visitor Guidance - Problem Re-Validation

Old Direction

Old idea: build an AR campus / tourist guide that shows location information, building introductions and navigation cues. The weakness was that this was solution-first and assumed AR value before proving a user problem.

New Problem-Focused Direction

Focus on new students and first-time visitors navigating unfamiliar university campuses, especially across indoor and outdoor environments. The problem hypothesis is that users may experience extra wayfinding effort because they must translate separate mobile maps, signs and verbal directions into real-world movement.

What Changed and Why

Decision for the Next Step

Retain AR campus guidance as the working scenario, but do not treat the solution as validated. Validate the navigation problem with secondary evidence and fresh user conversations. If users do not experience meaningful wayfinding difficulty, revise or drop the scenario.

Evidence Used

[1] Alghamdi et al. (2023), Applied Sciences 13(17), 9508 - campus wayfinding performance/workload.

[2] An ARCore-Based Augmented Reality Campus Navigation System (2021), Applied Sciences 11(16), 7515.

[3] Augmented Reality Indoor-Outdoor Navigation Through a Campus Digital Twin (CampusGo), IEEE CASCON.

Area | Before | After | Reason / Evidence

Target user | General campus users / tourists | New students and first-time campus visitors | Week 2 requires a narrow and explicit target user.

Problem | Need more campus information | Potential wayfinding difficulty and workload in unfamiliar indoor-outdoor campus environments | Campus wayfinding research directly studies navigation performance and workload [1].

Current alternatives | Not clearly defined | Google Maps, indoor navigation apps, campus signs/maps, websites, asking staff | Week 2 checkpoint requires current alternatives to be explicit.

Why AR | AR looks engaging | AR may reduce the need to translate a separate map into the physical environment | AR campus-navigation research gives a basis for testing this, not proof [2][3].

Scope | Campus + tourism + many features | Campus navigation/contextual guidance first; tourism kept as a later extension | Narrower scope is easier to test and prototype.

Success test | Build a working demo | Show the user problem exists first; later test whether AR changes time, workload, errors or confidence | Prevents a technology-first project.
