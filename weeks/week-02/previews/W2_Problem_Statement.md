Week 2 Problem Statement

Working scenario: Rokid AR Campus Navigation & Contextual Guide

Status: Working hypothesis for Week 2 problem re-validation. This document does not claim that the solution is validated.

1. Target User

Primary target user: new university students, visiting students, parents, guests and other first-time visitors who are unfamiliar with a large university campus.

2. Current Situation and Existing Alternatives

Users currently rely on outdoor map apps, indoor navigation systems where available, campus signs, static maps, websites, staff directions and trial-and-error wayfinding. Research on campus wayfinding shows that large campuses create indoor-outdoor navigation challenges and that different navigation aids can affect wayfinding performance and perceived workload [1].

3. Problem Hypothesis

New students and visitors on unfamiliar university campuses can experience difficulty translating screen-based or fragmented indoor/outdoor navigation information into the real physical environment, increasing wayfinding effort, uncertainty and reliance on repeated map checking or human directions.

4. Why the Problem Matters

Campus wayfinding is relevant to students, staff and visitors who need to locate classrooms, offices, laboratories and services. A 2023 field study compared Google Maps and MazeMap for indoor-outdoor campus navigation and explicitly examined wayfinding performance, perceived workload and design challenges in complex university environments [1].

A 2021 AR campus-navigation study reported that a mobile AR system combining real-scene overlays with indoor/outdoor navigation was evaluated on a university campus and showed better effectiveness/usability than the compared traditional systems in that study [2]. This supports further testing of in-context AR cues, but it does not prove that a Rokid-based solution will be superior for our target users.

5. Why AR May Be Relevant

AR can place arrows, destination markers and contextual building information directly in the user's field of view instead of requiring the user to repeatedly interpret a separate 2D screen. Rokid's supplied company material identifies AR navigation and on-site contextual information as typical application scenarios and describes its spatial-computing ecosystem as supporting AR development.

However, the project must test whether this spatial overlay creates enough real value to justify using AR glasses. A phone-based map may remain cheaper, more familiar and sufficient for many users.

6. Falsifiable Problem Statement

For new students and first-time campus visitors, navigating unfamiliar indoor-outdoor university environments may require repeated interpretation of separate maps, signs or directions; this problem is significant only if users experience measurable navigation difficulty or workload, and the hypothesis should be rejected or narrowed if fresh user evidence shows that existing maps/signage are already sufficient.

7. What Would Disprove or Weaken the Problem?

• Most new students/visitors report that existing maps and signs are already easy enough to use.

• Observed navigation tasks show little delay, confusion or repeated map checking.

• The main problem is not navigation but another issue (for example, event information or accessibility).

• Users strongly prefer phone navigation and see no meaningful benefit in in-view AR guidance.

8. Week 2 Validation Action

Use the three secondary sources listed below as the minimum evidence base, then add at least two fresh user conversations if possible. Ask users about recent navigation experiences rather than asking whether they 'like AR'. The goal is to validate the problem before finalising the solution.
