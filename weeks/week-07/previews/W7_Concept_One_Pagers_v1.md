Concept A Mobile Landmark Guide

ENT303TC  Table 26 Team 10  S1 W7  28 Oct 2026  Version 1

We propose a mobile web guide that links each route decision to a visible landmark. This is the leading option for the next small test, subject to team review of the comparison.

Mechanism  Mobile web software

User and problem

New students and first-time visitors need to decide which way to walk at unfamiliar junctions and entrances. The priority is local orientation rather than replacing every campus map.

Mechanism and user journey

Open a campus link or QR code, choose a destination, and follow a sequence of landmark photographs, short direction cues and entrance information. Keep a map available and show a clear recovery step when the user is unsure. These are proposed field features, not functions already proven on campus.

Value and evidence

EV-011 records an available map that did not settle walking direction. In EV-012 and EV-013, phone-held cues were associated with fewer checks or look-aways, from 9 to 4 and 11 to 2. P-03 nevertheless reported unchanged effort and confidence and no preference. The hypothesis is clearer decisions, not an established speed or workload benefit.

Prototype and delivery

The existing browser demo provides a simplified 3D scene, destination selection, map and in-view modes, HUD and spatial-arrow options, and viewpoint controls. It can explain the interaction. Real route content, mobile field use and reliable recovery still need checking. Unity modelling, GPS navigation and Rokid integration are not completed.

Commercial hypothesis and risks

An institution or event organiser could pay for route setup and content maintenance while visitors use the guide without a fee. Pricing and willingness to pay are untested. Main costs are route verification, content updates, hosting and support. Cue failures, stale content and useful existing maps remain reasons to change the concept.

Next evidence and comparison

Check one approved route and obtain task-based feedback on destination finding, cue understanding and recovery. Compare with Concept B before selecting a build. Proposed matrix score is 3.85 out of 5; this is an analytical judgement, not a participant result.

Concept B Decision Point Sign Kit

ENT303TC  Table 26 Team 10  S1 W7  28 Oct 2026  Version 1

We propose a small set of landmark-linked signs and a pocket map at confusing junctions and entrances. The mechanism changes the environment rather than asking the visitor to follow a screen.

Mechanism  Physical signage and printed information

User and problem

The same unfamiliar students and visitors need an immediate direction at a specific place. Clear building letters, room numbering and entrance identification may remove the need to interpret another app.

Mechanism and user journey

Locate difficult decision points with the facilities owner, create consistent arrows and landmark labels, and provide a simple printed route map. Visitors read the next direction in place without holding a device up. No campus-wide installation is assumed or authorised.

Value and evidence

P-01 found indoor signs useful and improved with familiarity (EV-011). P-02 valued a large physical map (EV-012). P-04 identified numbering complaints and facilities responsibility for signs (EV-014). These support a credible alternative; they do not demonstrate that a new sign kit would outperform the other concepts.

Prototype and delivery

No sign-kit prototype has been reported. A paper mock-up is technically simple and needs no positioning or AR stack. Installing or changing signs requires site permission, correct route information, accessible wording and an owner for maintenance.

Commercial hypothesis and risks

A facilities or event team could commission a scoped design and installation service with later updates. There is no confirmed buyer, quote or willingness to pay. Costs include site checks, design, printing, mounting and replacement. Physical space, weather, visual clutter and outdated destinations constrain usefulness.

Next evidence and comparison

Review one proposed decision point with facilities and compare a paper mock-up with the web-guide concept. The proposed score is 3.45 out of 5. It scores higher than A on technical ease, and becomes the leader if feasibility receives much more weight. Keep it as the active comparator.

Concept C Rokid HUD Route Guide

ENT303TC  Table 26 Team 10  S1 W7  28 Oct 2026  Version 1

We propose a Rokid glasses guide that presents the next direction and landmark in the wearer’s view. This retains a possible hardware route within the enterprise challenge, but the wearable build is deferred until its extra value and feasibility are demonstrated.

Mechanism  Wearable hardware and spatial guidance

User and problem

Unfamiliar visitors who find frequent phone handling inconvenient may benefit from visible directions while keeping their hands free. No participant has yet tested this project on glasses, so the size of this need remains unknown.

Mechanism and user journey

Select a destination and receive a short HUD direction and landmark label. Spatial arrows anchored to the environment are a separate future capability requiring appropriate tracking and device support. A safe fallback must handle missing cues and uncertainty.

Value and evidence

The phone-held cue observations in EV-012 and EV-013 make in-view directions plausible. P-03 disliked holding a phone up in crowds, but did not express a format preference. This is indirect evidence for considering wearable delivery, not proof of hands-free benefit, willingness to wear glasses or demand for Rokid hardware.

Prototype and delivery

The current browser demo shows presentation options only. The user confirms no Unity modelling. The demo states no GPS, Rokid SDK or glasses connection. Device access, the appropriate SDK, tracking, anchors, display legibility and safety must be established before a wearable implementation can be judged feasible.

Commercial hypothesis and risks

An institution might run loan devices for visitors or events. Device purchase, management, cleaning, charging, technical support and route maintenance would add cost. Budget ownership, pilot permission, user acceptance and pricing are all unconfirmed. More hardware does not by itself solve unclear landmarks or numbering.

Next evidence and comparison

Confirm the exact available device and supported development route, then determine whether a limited technical demonstration is justified. Keep browser interaction findings separate from wearer evidence. Proposed score is 1.35 out of 5. Defer this build while retaining it as a longer-term option.
