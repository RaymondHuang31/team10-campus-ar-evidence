Concept A Mobile Landmark Guide

ENT303TC  Table 26 Team 10  S1 W7  28 Oct 2026  Version 2

We compare a mobile-only guide, physical signs and a Rokid HUD guide. The current web AR demo is a transition prototype for demonstrating the intended glasses experience. Further Rokid HUD development remains the project goal.

Mechanism  Mobile web software

User and problem

New students and first-time visitors need to decide which way to walk at unfamiliar junctions and entrances. The priority is local orientation rather than replacing every campus map.

Mechanism and user journey

As a standalone alternative, visitors open a web link, choose a destination and follow landmark cues with a map fallback. In the chosen staged Rokid direction, the existing web demo is used to explain and check this interaction before device implementation. Real route delivery is still proposed.

Value and evidence

EV-011 records an available map that did not settle walking direction. In EV-012 and EV-013, phone-held cues were associated with fewer checks or look-aways, from 9 to 4 and 11 to 2. P-03 nevertheless reported unchanged effort and confidence and no preference. The hypothesis is clearer decisions, not an established speed or workload benefit.

Prototype and delivery

The browser demo provides a 3D campus, destination selection, map and in-view modes, HUD options and viewpoint controls. It supports presentation and interaction checks. It is a transition asset; it does not establish live campus positioning, mobile reliability or a working Rokid application.

Commercial hypothesis and risks

A mobile-only service could be paid for by an institution through route setup and content maintenance. This remains an alternative business hypothesis rather than the intended final product. Pricing, demand and maintenance ownership are untested.

Next evidence and comparison

Use the web prototype to check destination finding, cue understanding and recovery while progressing towards Rokid. A scores 3.85 for near-term readiness. This does not make it the final product choice. Retain a mobile-only guide as an alternative if later wearable evidence warrants a change.

Concept B Decision Point Sign Kit

ENT303TC  Table 26 Team 10  S1 W7  28 Oct 2026  Version 2

We propose a small set of landmark-linked signs and a pocket map at confusing junctions and entrances. The mechanism changes the environment rather than asking the visitor to follow a screen.

Mechanism  Physical signage and printed information

User and problem

The same unfamiliar students and visitors need an immediate direction at a specific place. Clear building letters, room numbering and entrance identification may remove the need to interpret another app.

Mechanism and user journey

Locate difficult decision points with the facilities owner, create consistent arrows and landmark labels, and provide a simple printed route map. Visitors read the next direction in place without holding a device up. No campus-wide installation is assumed or authorised.

Value and evidence

P-01 found indoor signs useful and improved with familiarity (EV-011). P-02 valued a large physical map (EV-012). P-04 identified numbering complaints and facilities responsibility for signs (EV-014). These support a credible alternative; they do not demonstrate that a new sign kit would outperform the other concepts.

Prototype and delivery

No sign-kit prototype has been reported. A paper mock-up is technically simple and needs no positioning or AR stack. Installing or changing signs requires site permission, correct route information, accessible wording and an owner for maintenance.

Commercial hypothesis and risks

A facilities or event team could commission a scoped design and installation service with later updates. There is no confirmed buyer, quote or willingness to pay. Costs include site checks, design, printing, mounting and replacement. Physical space, weather, visual clutter and outdated destinations constrain usefulness.

Next evidence and comparison

Keep B as a real comparator for the intended Rokid service. Its proposed readiness score is 3.45, and it leads when technical ease receives 60% weight. Review a paper sign mock-up if permitted. Evidence that signs solve the problem sufficiently should influence the wearable decision.

Concept C Rokid HUD Route Guide

ENT303TC  Table 26 Team 10  S1 W7  28 Oct 2026  Version 2

We intend to develop a Rokid glasses HUD guide. The web AR guide is the current transition demonstration. Progress will move from web interaction checks to a minimal device HUD and then a limited route trial, subject to technical and supervisory review.

Mechanism  Wearable HUD with staged device development

User and problem

Unfamiliar visitors who find frequent phone handling inconvenient may benefit from visible directions while keeping their hands free. No participant has yet tested this project on glasses, so the size of this need remains unknown.

Mechanism and user journey

Select a destination and see a short next-direction cue and landmark label. Start with a minimal HUD on the actual device. Environment-anchored arrows require a separate tracking and SDK check. Design a clear recovery step before attempting a larger route.

Value and evidence

The phone-held cue observations in EV-012 and EV-013 make in-view directions plausible. P-03 disliked holding a phone up in crowds, but did not express a format preference. This is indirect evidence for considering wearable delivery, not proof of hands-free benefit, willingness to wear glasses or demand for Rokid hardware.

Prototype and delivery

Device photos identify Rokid Max Pro Enterprise RA202 glasses and a Station Pro Enterprise RAS201. Power-on display is confirmed; the reported system is 3.2.21020240712102 / AOSP12. A project HUD application, SDK integration, live positioning and spatial anchors are not completed.

Commercial hypothesis and risks

An institution might provide managed loan glasses with route setup and software/content maintenance. Costs include hardware, charging, cleaning, support, hosting and updates. Buyer ownership, user acceptance, pricing and pilot permission remain unconfirmed.

Next evidence and comparison

Verify the supported SDK and build route, then demonstrate one readable HUD cue and controller input while stationary. If successful, plan a one-route wearer check of clarity, comfort and reliability. C retains its conservative readiness score of 1.35; the owner chose its staged development goal despite this gap, without claiming proven superiority.
